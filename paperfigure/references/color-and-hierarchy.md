# Color, hierarchy and recent-paper visual references

Use this when selecting a new figure's paint treatment, when its colors feel too
slide-like or uniform, or when a user asks to learn recent paper styles. For a
selected reference, retain the approved appearance unless a color/style revision
is authorized. A paper's acceptance is not an aesthetic score.

## Choose the encoding before the hues

Record the scientific categories and where they recur: modality, token/context
kind, actual parameter state, phase, or an interaction/objective. Choose which
visual channel carries each distinction. Keep explicit labels, direction and
line/shape cues; color alone must not invent frozen/trainable or positive/negative
semantics. Natural photo/video colors are data and are outside this scheme.

Decide separately how the encoding appears: small token/glyph accent, border,
connector, pale module surface, phase field or a localized dark core. A role color
need not become a fully saturated module fill. Make support visually quieter than
the central mechanism using area, contrast and typography as well as hue. Keep
labels dark on pale surfaces when appropriate; localized dark fills with readable
light text remain valid. Do not make every phase caption a prominent colored pill.

Inspect the actual figure at final paper width: can the focal relation still be
found, can text be read against its real surface, do repeated roles keep their
identity, and can labels/geometry still explain the distinction without hue?
Neither a set of hex values nor a contrast number establishes successful reading.
Do not adopt a fixed neutral/accent area ratio, a universal three-color limit, or
an alleged single “ICLR 2026 style.”

## Saved style resources

- [Visual style profiles](../assets/visual-styles.json): mechanism fit, composition,
  glyph/icon vocabulary, typography, borders/routes, examples and failure modes.
- [Optional palette families](../assets/palette-families.json): original treatments
  `soft-dual`, `plum-sage`, `line-first`, `token-categorical`. These are authored
  syntheses, not sampled author palettes, defaults or accessibility certifications.
- The profile index links to our own native/Office preview examples stored inside
  the skill. The paper links below supply external source inspiration; their
  figures are not bundled without reuse permission.

For a new version-1.1 ImageGen brief, express the selected treatment in its existing
`palette` meanings, block representations and typography instructions: name role
accent versus surface/stroke/text, the intended focal region, and support styling.
Review actual pixels for adherence. No new brief schema or prompt field is needed.
For an existing editable figure, a paint-only revision may reuse the reviewed
semantic plan and geometry. Preserve the original, record exact authorized paint
changes, renew the typography profile when text fills change, render through
Office and obtain independent review. Do not describe a new palette render as a
new composition search, ImageGen edit or new scientific case.

## What the sample actually supports

This is a purposive sample of **16 inspected method/architecture figures: 12 ICLR
2026 and 4 CVPR 2026 main-conference papers**. Eighteen papers were attempted in 20 logical download attempts;
MotionStream and U-Mind failed in two bounded attempts each and were replaced by
UltraMemV2 and FedAFD within the same venues. Availability and mechanism selection
bias the sample. No exhaustive survey, award ranking, venue-wide frequency claim,
reader study or superiority result is established.

Most useful here are semantic continuity, the distinction between role hue and
colored area, and dark structural ink on light supporting surfaces. Counterexamples
matter: MemAgent works mainly with line/color cues; LaVer and UltraMemV2 retain
localized dark blocks; MetaEmbed uses vivid token planes. Do not mute or recolor
those choices merely to imitate a fashion. Several figures are dense; learn their
color role without inheriting their small-font density or unsupported mechanisms.

The selected pages below were actually opened. LaVer's automatically suggested
benchmark figure was manually replaced by its method Figure 3; MemoryVLA and
MoAlign were also switched from teasers to method figures. Colors are qualitative
observations from pixels, not exact recovered palettes or inferred authoring tools.
